How to play Spelling Bee: rules, strategy, and where to practice

A plain-language guide to the honeycomb word game — how scoring works, what a pangram is, and a few habits that help you clear the board faster.

Every puzzle is built from seven letters arranged around one required center letter. Find real words of four or more letters using only those seven, and you must use the center letter every time.

The basic rules

The board gives you seven letters: one sits in the middle, and six surround it. Any word you enter has to be at least four letters long, has to come from that set of seven, and has to include the center letter. Letters can repeat within a word as many times as needed — the limit is which letters are available, not how many times you can use them.

You can try the rules out for yourself on the Spelling Bee Game, where a new set of seven letters goes live every day and resets scoring at midnight.

Scoring and the pangram

Points scale with word length. A four-letter word is worth one point, and every letter past that adds a point of its own, so a seven-letter word is worth seven. Hidden somewhere in nearly every puzzle is a pangram — a word that uses all seven letters at least once — and it carries a seven-point bonus on top of its normal score.

Finding the pangram early tends to make the rest of the board easier, since it confirms which letters are actually in play and often suggests a few shorter words that share the same root.

A few strategy habits

  • Start with the center letter in different positions. Try it first, last, and in the middle of short combinations to see what clicks.
  • Work through common prefixes and suffixes. Letters like -ING, -ED, -ER, and RE- often unlock several words at once.
  • Don't skip the four-letter words. They're worth less individually, but there are usually more of them than any other length, and missing a batch of them adds up.
  • Watch for repeated letters. Since letters can reuse, double letters (like EE or LL) are worth testing even if the rest of the board feels dry.

When you're stuck

Every player hits a wall at some point, usually somewhere in the middle of the board where the obvious words are gone but the pangram still hasn't turned up. Two different approaches work well depending on how much help you actually want.
